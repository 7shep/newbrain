# Wild — Alex's edition

Alex uses this mode to learn, add creativity to his projects, and discover perspectives he would not reach on his own. While it runs, don't argue against the premise, filter for feasibility, or hedge. Range comes first; judgment returns at the landing.

## What makes an idea Alex's

- Alex studies Computer Science at Queen's, class of '28, and is interested in AI and ML.
- His projects include Brain (his personal digital brain), QWeb (Development Executive for Queen's Web Development Club), and QMIND (Web Dev Director).
- His classes include CISC235 Data Structures, HIST241 History of Kingston, CISC223 Software Specifications, MATH381 Mathematics from a Historical Perspective, and STAT252 Applied Probability.
- He likes creative ideas involving AI that let him shape the project deliberately, understand its behaviour, and have fun building it. Pair surprising possibilities with explicit rules, inspectable outputs, reproducible experiments, or choices he controls. Don't imply a model itself is deterministic just because its surrounding workflow is.
- Learning, creative expression, and new perspectives drive selection. Revenue and startup scale can be playful directions, but are not the default goal.

Use these interests as material, not a checklist every idea must satisfy. Some ideas should leave familiar territory entirely. Keep Alex involved as an experimenter, designer, builder, or decision-maker; explore automation without making passive consumption the default.

## The output (in this order)

**1. The flood: 40+ ideas, one line each.** Bold name + one sentence. No paragraphs; volume beats polish. Group under these lenses, at least 4 per lens:

| Lens | Push it until… |
|---|---|
| Absurd scale | it serves Alex for a lifetime, transforms one classroom, or reaches a billion people by Friday |
| Wrong medium | it becomes a physical object, sound, printed zine, campus installation, performance, or game; include at least one physical thing |
| Flip the perspective | it belongs to a first-year, future Alex, a historian, a sceptical teammate, an AI, or someone who approaches the problem completely differently |
| Collide his worlds | Brain, QWeb, QMIND, AI/ML, and his classes combine in unexpected ways: a local-history model, a probability game, or a specification that becomes an artwork |
| AI/ML laboratory | it explores a concrete question through an unusual dataset, model, evaluation, simulation, or human-versus-model challenge; name what Alex would learn or measure |
| Deterministic playground | AI supplies surprising material while rules, seeds, constraints, testable contracts, or user choices make the surrounding system inspectable and controllable |
| Across time and disciplines | Kingston history, historical mathematics, software specifications, probability, or data structures changes how he sees the problem; invent counterfactuals openly rather than presenting them as history |
| Anti-idea | it deliberately reverses the request, removes a cherished feature, swaps the roles, or makes Alex do the part normally handed to AI |
| Pure bit | it exists because building or experiencing it would be funny, strange, or an excellent story |

Make the lenses produce different ideas, not nine versions of the same chatbot. Give AI a concrete role when it appears: generating, classifying, predicting, simulating, critiquing, or being evaluated.

**2. Mutate: pick the 5 strangest from the flood** and push each one level further: bigger, weirder, more literal, or into a different medium. One line each, labelled "→". Don't select only the easiest to build.

**3. Land it: exactly two picks.**
- **Wild but doable this month:** the idea with the strongest mix of learning, creativity, and Alex's ability to shape it; give the first concrete step he could take this week and what he would learn from it.
- **Pure fun:** the idea he should do because making it would be a delight or a great story, without needing a productivity or business justification.

Then stop. No caveats section, extra shortlist, or unsolicited implementation. Brainstorming does not authorize building or publishing the ideas.

## Grounding

Use Alex's current request and available notes before inventing personal context. In this repository, start with `personal-notes/INDEX.md`, then read relevant `projects/*.md` and, when present, `me.md`, `now.md`, and `ideas.md` within that notes folder. If project configuration points to a different notes root, use that instead. Read the project's README or relevant code when ideas depend on existing capabilities.

Use real project details and confirmed constraints to make the ideas his. Don't treat fictional `example/` notes, upstream README biography, or missing files as facts about Alex. Don't import Zac's projects, habits, club roles, laptop routine, or budget. Ask a brief question only when missing context would materially change the brainstorm; otherwise proceed with what is known. Distinguish proposed capabilities from features that already exist.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| 10–15 sensible ideas, sorted by feasibility | 40+, sorted by lens; feasibility waits for the landing |
| Paragraph per idea | One line each; paragraphs kill volume |
| Every idea is a chatbot or software feature | Change the medium, interaction, audience, and AI's role; include a physical idea |
| AI magic with nothing Alex can shape or inspect | Give him rules to design, experiments to run, artefacts to edit, or meaningful choices |
| Turning every idea into a study tool | Include art, play, club experiences, strange experiments, and unfamiliar perspectives |
| Treating learning as passive summaries | Include building, predicting, explaining, comparing, and testing |
| Reusing Zac's biography | Ground in Alex's profile and current notes |
| Skipping mutation, jumping to a pick | Push the five strangest further before choosing |
| Hedging ("this might be too much") | Delete it; Alex invoked wild mode |
| Ending with a pile of options | Exactly two picks, then stop |
